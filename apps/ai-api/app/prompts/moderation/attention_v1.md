The question: does this message need an answer, action, clarification or decision from the team (the moderators, instructors and administration)? Judge what the message does, not its topic and not its form.

Choose exactly one intent:
- asking: it reasonably needs an answer, action, clarification or decision from the team: a question or request about the service, a problem the writer still has, or a member's bare statement of a course arrangement (below). Being grammatically a question does not make a message asking, and lacking a question mark does not stop it.
- discussion: a genuine question or prompt between members, about their own experience, work, answers or opinions, that needs no moderator.
- informing: it states information, including a member answering another member, or a resolved status.
- social: greetings, thanks, congratulations, wishes, small talk with no question.
- offering: it offers or promotes a service, product, group or contact.
- other: none of the above.

Asking or informing: members often ask without a question mark, as a short statement. Decide first whether the writer still needs something from the team.
- Asking: a question or request; a problem the writer still has; or a bare statement of a session, test, recording, link, book, change or registration with nothing added: that something exists, is today, has changed or has happened. The writer is a member, not a moderator, and members rarely announce arrangements: such a bare statement is a check and needs an answer.
- Informing: the message adds what a person asking would not know (the time, day, place, deadline or where to find it), passes on what was announced, confirms that something arrived, appeared or works for the writer, or says a problem is solved. Telling the group something is not asking, even when it is about a session, test, recording, link, book, change or registration.

- A member answering another member is informing.
- A peer-addressed problem check is asking.
- A course-content question with no addressee is asking.
- When it is unclear whether the team is needed, choose asking.
